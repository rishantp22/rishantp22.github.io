BEHIND THE PLAYFUL SKYA LITTLE REAL SCIENCE

Space arrives
in small pieces.

NASA estimates that roughly 44 tonnes of meteoritic material enter Earth’s atmosphere each day. Across the whole planet, that average works out to:

Explore meteors & meteorites · NASA ↗

Approximate global mean mass · 1 tonne = 1,000 kg · Hourly, minute, and second values are rounded conversions of the daily estimate.

These values describe incoming mass across the entire Earth, not a count of visible meteors, material reaching the ground, or what you would see above one campsite. Estimates of the total infall vary.

Most of this incoming material vaporizes in the atmosphere. The ongoing infall of material does not mean a continuous visible meteor shower: showers occur when Earth travels through a stream of debris.
