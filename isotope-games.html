01 — THE METEORITE CHALLENGEFIVE SAMPLES / ONE SIMPLE EQUATION

Find the
epsilon signature.

For small differences, epsilon makes the digits easier to read. Try calculating ε54Cr for five fictional analyses.

THE RULE

ε54Cr = (x − 1) × 10,000

x = Rsample / Rreference
Here, R is 54Cr / 52Cr. The reference becomes x = 1; this does not mean the raw isotope ratio is 1.

✳These are fictional teaching values, already corrected consistently for mass fractionation. They are not measurements from named meteorites.

A worked example

If x = 1.000240, then ε54Cr = (1.000240 − 1) × 10,000 = +2.40. That same relative difference is +0.024% or +240 μ units.

A REAL EXAMPLE BEHIND THE EXPLORERSTARDUST, MEASURED

A tiny grain.
An extraordinary ratio.

GRAIN 2_37 · ORGUEIL METEORITE≈57×THE SOLAR 54Cr/52Cr RATIO

Nittler and colleagues reported a chromium-rich presolar grain about 60 nanometers across with a 54Cr/52Cr ratio of 1.6039 ± 0.1070 (1σ), compared with a solar value of 0.02821. That is about 56.86 times the solar ratio—approximately 57×.

Using the rounded value, 57× is 5,700% of the reference ratio and approximately 5,600% higher than it. The subtraction matters.

Not every presolar grain has a giant anomaly in every isotope ratio. These signatures depend on the grain and isotope system. This reported grain is a literature example, separate from the fictional meteorite challenge above.

References & the distinction that matters +

The meteorite game assumes consistently mass-fractionation-corrected chromium ratios, as used for nucleosynthetic ε54Cr comparisons. Epsilon is a scaling convention; multiplying by 10,000 does not perform the correction. The presolar example uses the grain-to-solar ratio reported in its own source and does not borrow the game’s bulk-meteorite correction convention.

The “Light leaves” experiment on the home page explores mass-dependent Rayleigh fractionation. A nucleosynthetic anomaly instead records differences in the stellar ingredients of a sample. The two ideas should not be confused.
